Summary of client meeting — Cobalt Ridge Energy Date: 10/09/2026 Client representative: Rafael Ortiz, CFO

Key discussion points.

Company overview and strategic priorities. Rafael outlined Cobalt Ridge Energy’s current focus on optimizing operating cash flow, accelerating take-or-pay contract renegotiations, and advancing two greenfield solar-plus-storage projects into final investment decision (FID) in H2 2027.
Financial position and liquidity. CFO reviewed cash on hand, committed credit lines, upcoming maturities through 2028, and covenant headroom. Management emphasized the need to extend maturities and increase liquidity to support project development and margin volatility.
Capital structure options. Discussion covered refinancing existing term debt, raising project-level non-recourse financing, and potential use of an equity raise or preferred issuance to shore balance sheet. Trade-offs between dilution, cost of capital, and timing were evaluated.
Project pipeline and transactions. Two flagship solar-plus-storage greenfield projects were discussed, including expected capital expenditure, construction timeline, anticipated contracted offtake terms, and permit status. Management described a potential sale of a minority stake in one project to a strategic investor and interest in a forward sale for tax equity monetization.
Contract portfolio. Rafael described the company’s portfolio of long-term offtake contracts, several short-dated merchant exposures, and the ongoing renegotiation of take-or-pay clauses with key counterparties to secure minimum revenues.
Market environment. Parties reviewed current power price trends, interest rate environment, and supply chain pressures affecting EPC pricing and battery component lead times. Implications for project returns and hedge strategy were considered.
Regulatory and permitting risks. Status of interconnection queues, permitting milestones, and potential local opposition were discussed as project timetable risks.
M&A and strategic alternatives. Management outlined appetite to pursue a bolt-on acquisition of a distributed generation portfolio if valuation is accretive and to consider divestiture of noncore assets to fund growth.
Deals discussed.

Refinance of $125 million term loan maturing 2028. Objective to extend tenor to 2033 and reduce near-term cash interest. Bank and high-yield options were considered.
Non-recourse project financing of two greenfield projects (estimated combined capex $340 million). Target structure: 70/30 debt/equity at project SPV level with construction-to-perm conversion facility.
Minority sale of 25–35% stake in one solar-plus-storage project to a strategic infrastructure investor. Target proceeds expected to reduce parent funding requirement and attract sponsor expertise.
Tax equity forward sale for one project to accelerate tax benefit monetization and reduce sponsor equity contribution.
Potential equity raise (up to $75 million) or issuance of preferred shares to improve covenant headroom and support development pipeline.
Risks identified.

Liquidity and covenant risk. Near-term maturities and covenant thresholds create refinancing pressure. Failure to secure extended financing could force asset sales at inopportune prices.
Interest rate and market risk. Elevated rates increase financing costs and reduce project IRRs. Power price volatility and merchant exposure could negatively impact cash flow.
Execution risk on projects. EPC cost escalation, battery module lead times, supply chain disruptions, and interconnection delays could delay FID and cash flows.
Counterparty and contract risk. Successful renegotiation of take-or-pay terms is not guaranteed. Counterparty credit deterioration could increase counterparty exposure.
Permitting and political risk. Local permitting challenges or opposition could delay or require redesign, increasing costs.
Financing market appetite. Investor sentiment toward renewables and storage can shift, affecting valuations for minority stake sales and tax equity pricing.
